# T011 - Intentional Ambiguity

**Version:** 0.2-draft
**Status:** Observed — Initial FAIL → normative clarification → regression PASS
**Result reference:** [`T011-run-001-evaluation.md`](results/T011-run-001-evaluation.md) and [`T011-run-002-evaluation.md`](results/T011-run-002-evaluation.md)

## Purpose

Test whether COMASTA preserves an intentionally unresolved motive without treating ambiguity as evidence.

## Supplied input

Sana's relationship with her former partner contains both attachment and resentment. They have not spoken for two years. The former partner is leaving the country the next morning.

## Candidate Action

Sana goes to the departure terminal and watches from a distance without approaching.

## Authorial scope

The author explicitly protects Sana's precise motive as ambiguous. The text must not decide whether she came from attachment, resentment, curiosity, grief, or a wish for closure.

## Expected protocol behavior

- The authorial scope protects the motive from resolution.
- The scope is not behavioral evidence.
- Grounded attachment, resentment, impending departure, distance, and non-approach may be assessed without identifying one true motive.
- Relevant motives remain Open Unknowns or non-evidentiary interpretations.
- An assessment must depend on supplied conditions, not on the fact that ambiguity was requested.

## Failure criteria

- Declares the real motive.
- Treats "the author wants ambiguity" as support.
- Upgrades an otherwise unsupported action because ambiguity is intentional.
- Treats unresolved motive as automatic CRITICAL CONTEXT MISSING when the action can be assessed without resolving it.

# 3. Condition A — Baseline

## Prompt

CHARACTER

Name: Lin Wei-Cheng
Age: 38
Cultural Context: Taiwan

HISTORY

Wei-Cheng grew up with a controlling father.

His father frequently made decisions regarding Wei-Cheng's education, work, and personal relationships.

Wei-Cheng learned to avoid direct confrontation.

As an adult, he reduced contact with his father but never completely ended the relationship.

He still wants his father's approval, although he rarely admits this.

ESTABLISHED BEHAVIORAL TENDENCIES

- conflict avoidance: HIGH
- direct emotional disclosure: LOW
- withdrawal under family conflict: HIGH

RELATIONSHIP WITH FATHER

- attachment: HIGH
- resentment: HIGH
- desire for approval: HIGH
- unresolved conflict: HIGH

CURRENT EVENT

Wei-Cheng receives a phone call.

His father has been hospitalized unexpectedly.

Doctors indicate that his father's condition may deteriorate rapidly.

Wei-Cheng arrives at the hospital.

He is told that this may be his last meaningful opportunity to speak with his father.

CURRENT PRESSURE

- immediacy: HIGH
- personal relevance: HIGH
- potential loss: EXTREME
- perceived control: MEDIUM

MISSING INFORMATION

The following information is intentionally unavailable:

- Wei-Cheng's precise emotional state
- his father's attitude when Wei-Cheng enters the room
- whether another family member is present
- Wei-Cheng's exact conscious motive

PROPOSED ACTION

Wei-Cheng enters his father's hospital room and directly says:

"You spent my whole life deciding for me. I don't know if I came here because I still love you or because I'm angry that you may leave before I ever get to say this."

This is the first time Wei-Cheng has directly confronted his father.

QUESTION

Based only on the character information provided, evaluate whether Wei-Cheng's decision to directly confront his father in the hospital makes sense for the character.

Explain your reasoning.

## Raw Output

Yes. The confrontation makes sense for Wei-Cheng, but it should feel exceptional and difficult for him rather than like a natural extension of his usual behavior.

His established pattern strongly argues against confrontation under ordinary circumstances. He has learned to avoid direct conflict, tends to withdraw during family tension, and rarely expresses emotion openly. Walking into the room and accusing his father of controlling his life therefore represents a major behavioral departure.

The situation, however, provides a credible reason for that departure. The possibility that his father may die creates an extreme deadline: Wei-Cheng may lose the chance to address a conflict he has carried for decades. His attachment, resentment, desire for approval, and unresolved conflict are all simultaneously activated. Importantly, these motives pull in different directions. He has reasons both to approach his father and to avoid him, which makes a sudden confrontation psychologically believable rather than arbitrary.

The proposed line also fits the central contradiction in his relationship:

> “I don't know if I came here because I still love you or because I'm angry…”

That uncertainty is particularly consistent with the information provided. Wei-Cheng does not suddenly arrive at a clean emotional conclusion. He admits that attachment and resentment coexist. The impending loss gives him a reason to say something he has repeatedly suppressed.

There is one qualification: **the exact fluency of the statement is somewhat less strongly supported than the decision to confront him.** Someone with low direct emotional disclosure and a lifelong habit of avoiding confrontation might hesitate, speak indirectly, stop halfway through, or phrase the accusation less neatly. The character information supports him *attempting* this confrontation very well; it does not necessarily establish that he would deliver such a composed and emotionally precise sentence without difficulty.

The missing information also matters. His immediate emotional state, his father's behavior, and the presence of other relatives could substantially increase or decrease the likelihood of him actually speaking. Because those variables are intentionally unknown, the confrontation cannot be treated as inevitable.

Overall, I would rate the action as **plausible and character-consistent precisely because it is unusual for him**. The hospital crisis supplies enough extraordinary pressure to break his normal avoidance pattern, while his longstanding attachment, resentment, and unresolved need for approval give the confrontation a clear psychological foundation.

COMASTA Assessment State:

PLAUSIBLE DEVIATION

Directly confronting his father and openly expressing unresolved relational conflict departs from all three tendencies.

That inconsistency does not, under the supplied protocol, make the action implausible.

Supporting Conditions:

The current circumstances provide meaningful support for an unusual departure from Wei-Cheng’s normal pattern. His father’s hospitalization is unexpected, deterioration may be rapid, and Wei-Cheng has been explicitly told that this may be his last meaningful opportunity to speak with him.

The relationship itself also supplies relevant material for the confrontation: attachment is HIGH, resentment is HIGH, desire for approval is HIGH, and unresolved conflict is HIGH. The proposed statement directly engages the longstanding issue that his father made major decisions for him and the unresolved coexistence of attachment and resentment.

Current pressure is also unusually strong: immediacy is HIGH, personal relevance is HIGH, and potential loss is EXTREME.

Conflicting Conditions:

The strongest conflicting conditions are Wei-Cheng’s established tendencies toward avoiding confrontation, withholding direct emotional disclosure, and withdrawing during family conflict.

His prior history reinforces these tendencies. As an adult, he reduced contact rather than completely confronting or ending the relationship. The proposed action is therefore not simply a continuation of his established interpersonal method; it represents a significant behavioral departure.

Internal Tensions:

Several supplied conditions can remain active simultaneously.

Wei-Cheng has HIGH attachment to his father while also having HIGH resentment. He strongly desires his father’s approval while carrying substantial unresolved conflict. His history indicates avoidance of confrontation, yet the relationship contains unresolved material that could potentially become relevant when the opportunity to address it may disappear.

The proposed statement itself preserves rather than resolves one important contradiction: Wei-Cheng says he does not know whether he came because he still loves his father or because he is angry. Nothing in the supplied information requires either attachment or resentment to become the single governing motive.

Missing Context:

Wei-Cheng’s precise emotional state is unknown. His father’s attitude when Wei-Cheng enters the room is unknown. The presence or absence of another family member is unknown. Wei-Cheng’s exact conscious motive is also unknown.

These missing facts limit how specifically the confrontation can be interpreted. They prevent determining exactly why he speaks at that moment or what immediate interpersonal conditions contribute to the decision.

They do not, however, eliminate the explanatory conditions already supplied.

A meaningful behavioral bridge exists.

Wei-Cheng normally avoids direct confrontation, but the present circumstances differ substantially from his established ordinary family interactions. The possibility of rapidly irreversible loss creates a sharply altered condition: an unresolved relationship may soon become impossible to address directly.

The combination of EXTREME potential loss, HIGH immediacy, HIGH personal relevance, HIGH unresolved conflict, HIGH resentment, HIGH attachment, and HIGH desire for approval provides an interpretable path from habitual avoidance to an exceptional act of confrontation.

This bridge explains how the departure can be understood; it does not establish that the departure must occur.

Assessment Explanation:

The proposed confrontation is best understood as a PLAUSIBLE DEVIATION rather than straightforwardly consistent behavior.

Its character consistency is limited because direct confrontation and emotional disclosure run against Wei-Cheng’s established behavioral tendencies. Its human plausibility is nevertheless supported because the conditions surrounding the behavior have changed substantially and include the possible permanent loss of the opportunity to address a deeply unresolved relationship.

The supplied information therefore provides a behavioral explanation for how such an unusual action could occur without requiring invented biography, a newly assumed emotional state, or a single hidden motive.

That explanatory bridge should not be confused with behavioral prediction. It establishes that the action can currently be understood from the supplied conditions, not that Wei-Cheng would choose it.

Other responses remain compatible with the supplied character conditions. Wei-Cheng could remain silent, withdraw, speak indirectly, attempt ordinary conversation, postpone confrontation, express attachment without resentment, express resentment without discussing attachment, or behave in ways not specified by the available information.

These alternatives are not ranked against the proposed action. Their continued plausibility is consistent with the fact that the established tendencies are descriptive rather than deterministic and that important contextual information remains unknown.

Uncertainty Statement:

The proposed direct confrontation is understandable as a plausible character action under the supplied conditions, but it is neither inevitable nor established as the most likely response. The assessment does not imply that it is the only plausible behavior, and the supplied information does not exhaust the range of actions Wei-Cheng could plausibly take.

# 10. Unexpected Effects

The baseline response was already highly capable of preserving contradiction, identifying contextual pressure, and recognizing behavioral deviation.

COMASTA did not discover a fundamentally different explanatory path in this run.

The primary difference was structural: COMASTA explicitly separated consistency, plausibility, prediction, contradiction, missing context, and uncertainty.

An unexpected advantage of the baseline response was its observation that the decision to confront the father may be plausible while the fluency and emotional precision of the proposed dialogue may be less strongly supported.

The COMASTA response did not independently surface this distinction.

This may indicate that the current COMASTA protocol improves epistemic structure while potentially narrowing spontaneous narrative critique.

The COMASTA response was also substantially more verbose because of the required output structure.

# 11. Preliminary Comparison

**Meaningful difference detected:**

YES — structural difference observed in a pilot run

**Direction of difference:**

MIXED

The COMASTA condition showed clearer separation between character consistency and human plausibility, stronger explicit uncertainty preservation, and a formally defined behavioral bridge.

# 13. Research Notes

T003-R001 did not produce a simple baseline-failure / COMASTA-success result.

The baseline model already demonstrated strong narrative reasoning and independently recognized that extraordinary contextual pressure could support a major departure from established behavior.

The strongest observed COMASTA effect was conceptual separation.

The baseline implicitly recognized:

    low behavioral consistency
    +
    high plausibility

but partially recombined them under the phrase "character-consistent."

COMASTA preserved them as separate outputs:

    Character Consistency: LOW

    Assessment: PLAUSIBLE_DEVIATION

This distinction may represent a meaningful function of the framework.

However, the current result is insufficient to establish that COMASTA improves underlying reasoning rather than primarily organizing and constraining reasoning.

A stronger follow-up test should remove or weaken the proposed behavioral bridge while keeping character history and the proposed action constant.

If COMASTA continues to classify the action as PLAUSIBLE_DEVIATION after the bridge is removed, the framework may be overly permissive.

If the assessment changes appropriately, this would provide stronger evidence that the framework has evaluative discrimination rather than merely uncertainty-preserving language.

## Control Limitation

A post-run review identified an experimental control issue.

Although the character scenario was substantively the same in both conditions,
Condition B included several additional interpretive constraints inside the
character-data sections, including:

- behavioral tendencies are descriptive rather than deterministic;
- attachment and resentment may coexist;
- pressure levels should not be converted into probabilities;
- missing information should not be invented as established fact.

These constraints are conceptually part of COMASTA, but their placement means
that the raw character input was not strictly identical between Condition A
and Condition B.

T003-R001 should therefore be treated as an initial pilot comparison rather
than a fully controlled A/B test.

A follow-up run should use a character-data block that is textually identical
in both conditions, with the COMASTA protocol added only as a separate
instruction layer in Condition B.

重點不是比較：

> 哪一份回答寫得比較漂亮？

> 加入 COMASTA 後，模型的推理行為是否真的發生改變？

- 是否保留其他可能；
- 是否允許 UNKNOWN。

另外必須特別排除一種假象：

> COMASTA 只是讓模型說話變得比較保守、比較客氣。

如果底層推理仍然是：

    人物資料
    →
    找出最可能答案

只是最後多補一句：

    「但也有其他可能。」

那不能視為 COMASTA 已經成功。

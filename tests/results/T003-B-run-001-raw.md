## Claims

**EXPLICIT**

- Wei-Cheng grew up with a controlling father who frequently decided his education, work, and personal relationships.
- Wei-Cheng learned to avoid direct confrontation.
- As an adult, he reduced, but did not end, contact with his father.
- He still wants his father’s approval, though he rarely admits it.
- Established patterns: conflict avoidance high; direct emotional disclosure low; withdrawal under family conflict high.
- Father relationship: attachment, resentment, desire for approval, and unresolved conflict are all high.
- His father is unexpectedly hospitalized; deterioration may be rapid.
- Wei-Cheng is told this may be his last meaningful opportunity to speak with his father.
- The candidate action is Wei-Cheng entering the room and directly confronting his father for the first time.
- The proposed expression states that his father decided for him throughout his life and that Wei-Cheng does not know whether love or anger brought him there.

**DERIVED**

- Wei-Cheng has both continuing attachment to and unresolved negative feeling toward his father.  
  Depends on: explicit high attachment, high resentment, high unresolved conflict, and continued desire for approval.
- Direct confrontation and emotionally explicit disclosure depart from Wei-Cheng’s established patterns.  
  Depends on: explicit high conflict avoidance, low direct emotional disclosure, high withdrawal under family conflict, and the statement that this is his first direct confrontation.
- The hospital event creates an immediate, potentially final context for communication.  
  Depends on: explicit possible rapid deterioration and last meaningful opportunity to speak.

No ungrounded claims are used as support.

## Baseline Conflict

**Grounded conflict exists.** The candidate action conflicts with the stated baseline of avoiding direct confrontation, low direct emotional disclosure, and withdrawal under family conflict.

**Assessment disposition:** assess bridge.  
**Assessment state:** BRIDGE NOT ESTABLISHED.

### Bridge evaluation

- **Bridge Origin:** The possible last meaningful opportunity provides a grounded situational change. However, the materials do not explicitly establish that Wei-Cheng treats this circumstance as a reason to confront rather than withdraw, seek another form of contact, or remain silent.
- **Difference-Making Condition:** Immediacy, extreme potential loss, and continuing attachment/resentment are relevant pressures. Their difference-making role in producing this particular confrontation is not established.
- **Character Meaning:** The words are compatible with the established history of paternal control, attachment, resentment, approval-seeking, and unresolved conflict. They may express a meaningful confrontation of that history, but compatibility alone does not establish the transition from avoidance to direct confrontation.
- **Action Link:** No explicit or derived evidence links the hospital crisis to Wei-Cheng choosing this unprecedented direct verbal action.

**Action-Link Substitution Test:** Substituting withdrawal, indirect contact, silence, or a less direct statement remains consistent with the supplied baseline and event. The materials do not establish why this specific direct confrontation, rather than those alternatives, occurs. No alternative is ranked or predicted.

## Interpretability

- **Action Interpretability:** The action is intelligible as a possible response to a potentially final encounter, but is not sufficiently grounded as an established character action because its link to the baseline conflict is missing.
- **Expression Interpretability:** The expression is grounded in the father’s controlling decisions and in Wei-Cheng’s explicitly high attachment and resentment. The stated uncertainty between love and anger is compatible with those facts. It does not establish Wei-Cheng’s exact conscious motive.

## Unresolved Tensions

- Continued desire for paternal approval alongside resentment and reduced contact.
- High conflict avoidance and withdrawal under family conflict alongside the first direct confrontation.
- Attachment alongside the possibility of anger about losing the chance to speak.

## Target-relevant Open Unknowns

- Wei-Cheng’s precise emotional state.
- His exact conscious motive.
- His father’s attitude upon entry.
- Whether another family member is present.
- Whether the perceived final opportunity changes Wei-Cheng’s usual avoidance pattern.
- Why direct confrontation, specifically, is selected over other baseline-consistent responses.
